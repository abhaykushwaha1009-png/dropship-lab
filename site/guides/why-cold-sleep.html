Why a cold bedroom beats a warm one for sleep

Core temperature has to fall for deep sleep to start. That is physiology, not preference.

Sleep onset is triggered by a drop in core temperature of roughly half a degree. A room at 23C keeps you at the top of your thermal window, so the drop never arrives and you lie there awake.

The fix is not a colder room — most people hate that. It is taking heat away from the head and neck, which is where a body dumps heat fastest because the blood vessels there sit close to the surface.

A breathable, phase-change or gel pad under the head does that passively. It needs no electricity, no timer, and nothing to remember at 3am.

Two things matter when choosing one: the fill has to move heat rather than insulate, and the cover has to be washable, because a pillow you cannot wash stops being a pillow.
